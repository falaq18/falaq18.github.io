idea pressure test

a web app that grills your startup idea before you fall in love with your own pitch deck.

16 questions13 evaluations7 lenses35/30/35

16 uncomfortable questions across three phases (the guru test, the founder test, the reality test) backed by 13 evaluations drawn from seven startup lenses: hormozi, graham, altman, thiel, the amodeis, naval and demand-first.

each lens scores the idea out of 10 as trash, contender or jackpot. weighted 35/30/35 across phases, it hands you a blunt verdict (build it, pivot it or bin it) plus a concrete 30-day execution plan if it survives.

applypilot

a multi-agent job-hunting engine that refuses to lie for you.

9 agents24 scripts375 testsnever hits submit

a system a few friends and i built to take the repetitive admin out of job hunting. 9 agents search job boards in parallel, score role fit, tailor resumes, prep interview briefs and draft outreach, while a shared sheet tracks the pipeline.

the core constraint: automation shouldn't make you dishonest. nothing goes onto a draft unless it already exists in your ground-truth history, and an independent auditor agent checks every single resume before you see it.

property, tokenised

dual-arm fractional real estate: from co-owning luxury vacation villas and commercial land to programmable ownership rails in india.

v1 · fix co-ownership offline first

multiple investors co-own high-yield vacation villas or commercial land parcels through a clean legal structure. the platform runs the unglamorous stuff first: spv records, rental yield splits, maintenance reserves, co-owner voting and secondary exits.

once the trust works, upgrade the rails
v2 · tokenisation

legally enforceable spv equity or rental rights get represented as permissioned digital tokens, so transfers, cap-table updates and compliance checks happen cleanly on-chain where the law allows.

non-negotiables: no speculative coin, no fake "instant liquidity" promises, no guaranteed returns. legal enforceability before code.

01 · the big one

the income ladder & the consumption cascade

it started withthe standard economist take: when countries cross a certain gdp per capita, consumption explodes. just look at china, south korea, japan and taiwan.

my objectionwhy are we talking about countries as averages? india already has every income bracket living side by side right now: someone earning ₹10,000 a month, a software engineer on ₹2 lakh, a founder on ₹20 lakh. the future of india isn't hiding in another country's past. it's already visible in how richer indians spend today.

the better questioninstead of asking "what do people buy as they get richer?", ask: "which single purchase sets off a chain reaction?"

income ↑ · read it bottom to top
5experiencethe value is memory, access and identity, not utilitytravel, wellness, fine dining, luxury
4convenience"is my time worth more than the fee?"blinkit, instamart, urban company, premium healthcare
3identity"who am i becoming?"iphone, nike, zara, cult
2aspiration"what does this say about me?"smartphone upgrade, eating out, better wardrobe
1survival"can i afford this?"food, rent, transport, utilities
one anchor purchase, then the mandatory chain reaction
iphone→airpods→apple watch→macbook→icloud→apple pay→subscriptions
₹40L flat → ₹1.5Cr apartment→interior designer→custom furniture→modular kitchen→luxury lighting→smart locks→housekeeping→international schools nearby
hedonic ratchet

once you get used to 10-minute groceries on blinkit, walking to the store doesn't feel neutral. it feels painful.

diderot effect

one upgrade makes everything else you own look out of place. your identity demands coherence.

the core unlock

consumers don't upgrade products. they upgrade identities.

where i landed
income rises→identity expands→anchor purchase→consumption cascade→lifestyle locks in→permanent spending floor rises

that's why consumption in india doesn't grow in a polite straight line. it steps up in cascades.

the founder-fit multiplier

it started withwhile designing a system to evaluate startup ideas, i hit a wall: most vc frameworks obsess over market size. yet mediocre ideas routinely make millions while brilliant ideas die every week. why?

the insightbecause evaluating an idea in a vacuum is useless. same idea, different founder, completely different probability of survival. so i took the best market & product filters: graham (desperate need), thiel (contrarian truth), hormozi (immediate willingness to pay), naval (leverage) and altman (scale). then i bolted on the missing variable: founder fit.

where it wentthis became the backbone of idea pressure test, where 30% of the entire score comes down to the founder test.
